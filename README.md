# Sourdough Recipes

Recipes and short summaries built on one idea: **let enzymes do the work while the yeast sleeps.**

Every recipe here uses the same low-effort workflow. A levain is fed once, parked in the
fridge for the week, and pulled out whenever a bake fits the schedule. The dough gets a long
cold rest *before* bulk fermentation, so amylase has hours to turn starch into sugar while
the microbes are dormant. When the dough warms up, the yeast wakes to a buffet, bulk goes
fast, and the crust browns deeply.

No daily feeding. No discard. No planning a day around a peak.

---

## TL;DR of the method

1. **Feed levains once a week.** Small jars, 1:2:2, into the fridge. Use within about five days.
2. **Wake the levain in the dough's own ingredients.** Dissolve it cold in the recipe water and
   whisk in some of the flour. Wait about an hour for bubbles.
3. **Mix the final dough and refrigerate it straight away** for 8 to 16 hours. This is the
   enzymatic rest: amylase makes sugar, protease barely touches the gluten because the pH is
   still high.
4. **Bulk the next day.** One stretch-and-fold, then a few coil folds over 3 to 4 hours. Bulk is
   shorter than usual because the dough is already primed with sugar.
5. **Shape, cold proof if you like, bake with steam then dry.**

## Conventions

- **Baker's percentages** on the dough flour. Levain is listed separately and is not folded
  into the flour or water totals unless stated.
- **Temperatures in °C** with °F in parentheses.
- **Levain is 100% hydration** unless a recipe says otherwise.
- Steps that say "whenever you can" mean it. The cold phases are pauses, not deadlines.
